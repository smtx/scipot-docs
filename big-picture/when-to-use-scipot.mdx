The most useful thing a docs page can do is help you decide whether to *not* use the product. Saves you a week.

So: when is SciPot the right answer, and when isn't it?

---

## Use SciPot when…

### 1. Your agents make decisions that have consequences

If your AI is recommending which trade to execute, which patient protocol to follow, which contract clause to accept, or which engineering decision to make — you need to know *how much to trust* what it says.

A confident-sounding answer from a vanilla RAG pipeline is dangerous in these contexts. SciPot's POT Score tells your agent (and the human reviewing the agent) exactly how confident the underlying knowledge is. A 0.95 fact and a 0.42 fact get treated differently, automatically.

**Smell test:** Could a wrong answer cost more than $1,000 in money, time, reputation, or compliance risk? → SciPot.

### 2. Your domain has experts whose judgment matters

If there are people in your organization whose word actually *counts* on certain topics — the CFO on pricing, the lead engineer on architecture, the head of legal on compliance — SciPot treats them as Human Curators with first-class API support.

They can promote facts to Constitutional level. They can resolve contradictions. They can approve or disapprove extracted facts with audit-trailed records. Their input doesn't get washed away by the next extraction batch.

**Smell test:** Is there at least one person whose answer you'd take over the LLM's, on at least one topic? → SciPot.

### 3. Your knowledge base has internal contradictions

If you're consolidating Slack threads, Notion pages, customer interviews, decision memos, post-mortems — there *will* be contradictions. The 2024 pricing memo says $99. The 2025 pricing update says $129. The sales team is quoting $119. All three exist in your data.

A typical RAG pipeline averages them into a fluent answer with no flag. SciPot detects the contradiction (via typed epistemic edges), flags it explicitly, and either escalates to a Curator or surfaces the conflict to the calling agent.

**Smell test:** Do you have multiple sources that disagree, and does it matter which is right? → SciPot.

### 4. You need an audit trail

Compliance, due diligence, internal reviews, post-mortems — sometimes you need to answer "where did this answer come from?" two months after the fact. Vector stores can't tell you that. They store chunks; they don't store *attribution at the fact level*.

SciPot's provenance is a first-class field on every fact. Source document, extraction method, who curated it, when it was promoted to Verified — all in the audit trail.

**Smell test:** If someone asked "why did the agent say that?" six months from now, do you need to be able to answer? → SciPot.

### 5. You're betting on agents that get *better* over time

If your goal is agents that improve as your team uses them — incorporating new decisions, refining their understanding of edge cases, learning what the human Curators consistently approve or disapprove — SciPot is built for that. It has a contribution surface (`POST /facts/contribute`), a curation surface (`POST /facts/{id}/curate`), and expertise-based scoring that rewards accurate contributors.

**Smell test:** Do you imagine your agent being smarter in 6 months than it is today, *because of how your team has used it*? → SciPot.

## Don't use SciPot when…

### 1. You just need stateless single-turn responses

If your AI agent is essentially a polished chatbot — user asks, agent answers, conversation ends — and you don't need memory across sessions, you don't need SciPot. Use the foundation model's built-in context window.

**The honest reason:** SciPot's overhead (ingestion pipeline, score propagation, edge detection) is only worth it if facts persist and accumulate.

### 2. You need pure semantic search over a static corpus

If your use case is "I have 10,000 product docs and I want users to search them with natural-language queries," that's textbook RAG. Use Pinecone, Weaviate, or pgvector. They're cheaper, faster, and equally good for that specific shape.

**The honest reason:** SciPot adds value when retrieval needs to be *justified*. If "just return the most semantically similar chunks" is the actual product requirement, SciPot is over-engineered.

### 3. You only need conversation memory

If you want "remember what the user said earlier in this thread" — that's session memory, not persistent organizational knowledge. Mem0 and Zep are specifically optimized for that shape; they have better SDK ergonomics, simpler setup, and lower latency for chat-shaped data.

**The honest reason:** SciPot is designed for facts that outlive a single conversation. If your memory horizon is hours, not months, the wrong tool is in your hand.

### 4. You're prototyping and setup time matters more than trust

If you're hacking together a demo for a Friday investor pitch, SciPot's full setup (POT creation, Constitution authoring, ingestion, edge detection) is friction you don't need. OpenAI File Search will get you a working demo in 20 minutes.

**The honest reason:** SciPot's value is most visible in production, on real data, over months. In a 48-hour hackathon, that value won't have time to materialize.

### 5. You don't have a human-in-the-loop available

SciPot's full power requires at least one Human Curator — someone who can write the Constitution, resolve contradictions, and approve high-stakes facts. If your operational reality is "no one will ever look at this; it has to be fully autonomous," SciPot will still work, but you're paying for capability you can't activate.

**The honest reason:** The Agentic Curator handles ~80% of maintenance. The remaining 20% — the part that determines whether the system stays trustworthy as it grows — needs a human.

## Borderline cases (and what tips them)

### "We're building an internal knowledge assistant for our 50-person team"

- **SciPot** if you want every employee's contributions tracked, contradictions surfaced, and your VP of Eng's word to actually count more than a Slack thread.
- **Notion AI / Glean** if you want polish-over-precision and don't have anyone to curate.

### "We're building a developer-facing AI agent that answers questions about our API"

- **SciPot** if your API surface has multiple versions, deprecated endpoints, and edge cases where being subtly wrong is dangerous.
- **OpenAI File Search** if your docs are clean, versioned, and you trust the LLM to handle disambiguation.

### "We're building a customer support bot"

- **SciPot** if support quality is a revenue driver and you have a head of CX who curates answers from real tickets.
- **Mem0 + a vector store** if support is a cost center and "good enough" answers are the bar.

### "We're building an analytics agent that reasons over our data warehouse"

- **SciPot** for the *semi-structured business knowledge* layer (definitions, policies, decisions) the agent needs to reason about.
- **Your warehouse + Text-to-SQL** for the actual data queries. These are complementary, not competing.

---

## The honest meta-answer

SciPot is opinionated about a specific shape of problem: **production AI agents operating on organizational knowledge, with humans in the loop, where trust and audit matter**.

It is not a vector DB. It is not a chat memory. It is not a wiki. It is not a data warehouse. It is the *layer between* your data and your agents — where facts get scored, sources get tracked, contradictions get flagged, and humans get to weigh in on what counts as canon.

If that shape matches your problem, SciPot is differentiated and worth the setup time. If it doesn't, we'll point you at the right tool. We've been honest above; we'll be honest in support.
