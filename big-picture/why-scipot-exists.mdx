## The problem nobody wants to name

Organizations have more information than ever. More AI tools than ever. More compute than ever.

And yet:

- Every time someone leaves, they take critical knowledge with them.
- Every new project starts from zero.
- Every important decision depends on finding the right person at the right moment.

**Organizational intelligence doesn't accumulate. It evaporates.**

The market is obsessed with bigger models, faster copilots, more elaborate prompts. But:

- A giant model without context is still generic.
- A copilot without memory still starts from zero every morning.
- **95% of enterprise GenAI pilots don't generate measurable P&L impact** ([MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide", August 2025](https://nanda.media.mit.edu/ai_report_2025.pdf)).
- **Over 40% of AI Agent projects will be cancelled by 2027** due to costs, lack of clear ROI, or inadequate controls ([Gartner, June 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)).

The problem isn't the technology. **The problem is that intelligence has nowhere to live.**

> "The competitive advantage is no longer in *using* AI. It's in *structuring where and how it thinks*."

The first wave was "AI that responds." The second wave is **AI that remembers, learns, and specializes**.

Stripe solved payments. Twilio solved communications. **SciPot solves memory for AI agents.**

Memory-as-a-Service: the missing infrastructure that makes the 95% work.

## What's actually different

Today's AI agents are like employees with amnesia. Every conversation starts from zero. Without persistent memory, there's no context. Without context, there's no business value.

The fixes the industry has tried — vector stores, RAG pipelines, conversation memory, file search — all share the same blind spot: **they retrieve information without ever telling you how much to trust it**.

A vector store returns "chunks similar to your query." A typical RAG pipeline returns "documents that probably mention the topic." OpenAI File Search returns "passages from your uploaded files." None of them tell you:

- **Where did this fact actually come from** (a specific paragraph in a specific document, written by whom, on what date)?
- **How certain is the system** that this fact is true (0.85 vs 0.42 is a meaningful difference)?
- **Does any other fact in the knowledge base contradict it** (and if so, was the contradiction surfaced or silently ignored)?
- **Who decided this fact belongs in the canon** (an extraction LLM at 2am, or a Human Curator with domain authority)?

SciPot answers all four. The unit isn't a chunk or a passage — it's a **scored, sourced, contradiction-aware fact** living inside a knowledge container (a POT) with explicit foundational axioms (a Constitution) and a clear workflow for curation.

That's the difference between "retrieval" and **memory you can trust in production**.

## What is a Company Brain?

A **Company Brain** is a SciPot POT scoped to an organization's knowledge: pricing decisions, hiring policies, post-mortems, product roadmaps, customer feedback, internal processes. It's the canonical source the company's AI agents query before they act.

Unlike a wiki, a Company Brain:

- **Knows what it knows, and what it doesn't.** Every fact has a POT Score.
- **Knows where it learned each fact.** Every score has a source.
- **Knows when two of its beliefs disagree.** Every contradiction is flagged for a Human Curator.
- **Evolves under governance.** New facts go through extraction, scoring, edge detection, and (when stakes are high) Human Curator approval — not "save and pray."

A Company Brain doesn't replace your wiki, your CRM, or your data warehouse. It sits on top of them, **as the trust layer your agents use to reason**.

## The four retrieval strategies

SciPot uses a **Hybrid Router** that intelligently composes four retrieval strategies, picking the best one — or combination — for each query:

| Strategy | Best for | Latency |
|---|---|---|
| **CAG** (Cached Augmented Generation) | Factual lookups with known entity references | &lt;5 ms |
| **RAG** (Retrieval Augmented Generation) | Semantic queries, paraphrased questions | ~200 ms |
| **GraphRAG** | Relational queries that traverse edges between facts | ~500 ms |
| **DocumentIndex** | Hierarchical document reasoning (coming) | ~1 s |

Each strategy returns facts ranked by a combined score: `combined = (similarity × 0.7) + (pot_score × 0.3)`. The Hybrid Router classifies the incoming query and chooses the optimal strategy automatically — or you can pin a strategy explicitly when you have a preference.

## The wager

SciPot is a wager on two beliefs:

1. **Memory is the unsolved layer of the agentic economy.** Models keep getting better. Tools keep getting cheaper. The thing that hasn't been figured out is *where AI's working knowledge lives, how it's validated, and how it survives the people who created it*. That's a category — not a feature.

2. **Trust is the moat.** Anyone can store text. Anyone can return chunks. Few systems can return facts with receipts, detect contradictions, and let humans govern their own canon. That's the work. That's SciPot.

If those two beliefs are right, SciPot becomes infrastructure the same way Stripe became infrastructure: invisible when working, indispensable in retrospect.

If they're wrong, you'll know in 18 months because someone will have built a better answer. We're betting that no one will, because nobody else is starting from "every fact has a score, every score has a source, every contradiction is flagged."
