Curation is the deliberate, ongoing work of keeping a Company Brain accurate, calibrated, and aligned with the organization's actual knowledge. SciPot splits it into two roles:

| Role | What it does | When it acts | Where the buck stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Agentic Curator** | Ingestion, extraction, edge detection, score propagation, obvious contradiction flagging | Continuously (24/7), reactively, on every write | No — its outputs are reviewable and reversible |
| **Human Curator** | Constitution governance, complex contradiction resolution, fact promotion to VERIFIED, expertise-weighted contributions | When the Agentic Curator surfaces something or when a domain expert volunteers a fact | **Yes** — Human Curator decisions are the system's ground truth |

Both roles share a fact graph. Both produce `CurationRecord` audit entries. The difference is authority: the Agentic Curator approximates; the Human Curator decides.

## The Agentic Curator

The Agentic Curator is not a single agent — it's a set of automated services that run inside SciPot:

- **Ingestion pipeline.** Documents come in (PDF, DOCX, MD, plain text), get chunked, get fact-extracted by an LLM, get embedded, get persisted with initial scores.
- **Edge detection.** After ingestion, the system detects relationships between facts: SUPPORTS, CONTRADICTS, REFINES, DERIVES_FROM, plus structural edges (IS_PART_OF, IS_TYPE_OF) and others.
- **Score propagation.** When a new fact lands, scores ripple through the graph: supports boost their targets, contradicts get flagged, derives_from caps its derived facts.
- **Contradiction triage.** Obvious contradictions (clear, atomic, complementary claims) get flagged with severity. Complex ones get surfaced to Human Curators.
- **Decay management.** Time-sensitive facts (those with non-zero λ) get their scores updated on read.

You don't usually invoke the Agentic Curator directly — it runs as a side effect of every ingestion, every edge detection call, every fact write. But you *can* trigger specific operations:

```bash
# Detect edges between specific facts (intra-POT)
curl -X POST "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/edges/detect" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "fact_ids": ["uuid-a", "uuid-b", "uuid-c"],
    "include_constitution": true,
    "auto_save": true
  }'

# Detect edges between every extracted fact in the POT
# (expensive — n*(n-1)/2 LLM calls — but exhaustive)
curl -X POST "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/edges/detect-intra" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY"
```

The Agentic Curator's outputs are not silently authoritative. Every action produces a record that a Human Curator can audit, approve, or override.

## The Human Curator

A Human Curator is a human (or a team of humans) with authority over a POT's canon. Typical roles in an organization:

- **CTO** for the Engineering Brain.
- **Head of Sales** for the Sales Brain.
- **CFO** for the Pricing/Financials Brain.
- **Head of Legal/Compliance** for the Compliance Brain.

A Human Curator's powers:

1. **Promote facts to higher levels** — push an EXTRACTED fact to VERIFIED (or all the way to CONSTITUTION) based on independent knowledge.
2. **Demote or delete facts** that an extraction got wrong.
3. **Resolve contradictions** — choose which fact survives when two collide, mark one as superseded, or split them with refinement edges.
4. **Amend the Constitution** — the only role that can.
5. **Approve or reject contributed facts** from non-curator users.

Every Human Curator action creates a `CurationRecord` audit entry: who did what, when, with what rationale, and (for amendments) what the prior state was.

### Curate an extracted fact

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/facts/{fact_id}/curate" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "action": "approve",
    "promote_to_level": "VERIFIED",
    "curator_id": "user_cto_001",
    "rationale": "Confirmed against ADR-2026-014; this is the canonical retry policy."
  }'
```

Or disapprove:

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/facts/{fact_id}/curate" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -d '{
    "action": "disapprove",
    "curator_id": "user_cto_001",
    "rationale": "Extraction confused two products; the deprecated policy applies only to v1."
  }'
```

The result: the fact is updated, the audit trail records the action, and downstream propagation re-runs.

## Contributors (M20+) — humans who aren't full Curators

Not everyone in an organization is a Curator, but lots of people have useful knowledge. SciPot supports a **Contributor** role: trusted humans who can submit new facts that the system scores based on their declared expertise.

```bash
curl -X POST "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/facts/contribute" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -H "Content-Type: application/json" \
  -d '{
    "content": "Our default Redis eviction policy is allkeys-lru, set in the deploy script line 47.",
    "domain": "infrastructure",
    "contributor_id": "user_eng_017",
    "contributor_expertise": {
      "infrastructure": 0.9,
      "data_engineering": 0.4
    },
    "sources": [
      {
        "title": "deploy/redis/redis.conf",
        "section": "line 47",
        "reliability": "high"
      }
    ]
  }'
```

The initial score is calculated from the contributor's declared expertise in the relevant domain, with diminishing returns for repeat contributions:

```
contribution_score = base_score × expertise_factor × diminishing_returns_factor
```

A Human Curator can review the contribution and either approve (promote to VERIFIED), leave it (extracted), or disapprove.

The expertise-based scoring is what prevents two failure modes:

1. **Spam by enthusiastic non-experts.** Someone who claims expertise in everything gets diminishing returns until a Curator validates their judgment.
2. **Silencing real experts.** Someone who has consistently been approved in a domain has their contributions scored higher by default — they earn weight by being right.

## ContributorProfile — earned authority

Every contributor accumulates a `ContributorProfile` over time:

```typescript
ContributorProfile: {
  user_id: string,
  domains: {
    [domain: string]: {
      declared_expertise: number,        // 0.0–1.0, self-reported
      contributions_count: number,
      approval_rate: number,             // % approved by Human Curators
      effective_expertise: number,       // derived: weighted by approval_rate
      last_active: string
    }
  },
  global_approval_rate: number,
  total_contributions: number
}
```

Effective expertise drifts toward earned authority over time. A contributor who declared 0.9 expertise in `infrastructure` but has a 30% approval rate ends up with effective expertise around 0.27. Someone who declared 0.4 but has a 95% approval rate ends up around 0.38, possibly higher with bonus weighting.

The system rewards being right, not being loud.

## Audit trail — every curation action is recorded

```typescript
CurationRecord: {
  id: string,
  pot_id: string,
  fact_id: string,                       // or null for Constitution actions
  action: "approve" | "disapprove" | "promote" | "demote" | "amend_constitution" | "resolve_contradiction",
  prior_state: object,                   // for amendments
  new_state: object,
  curator_id: string,
  rationale: string,                     // free text, required for non-trivial actions
  timestamp: string,
  related_records: string[]              // links to prior records, contradicting facts, etc.
}
```

Six months from now, when someone asks "why did the agent claim X?" — you can trace it: the fact's provenance points to the source, the curation record points to who approved it, the contradiction resolution points to what was rejected and why.

That's the difference between a knowledge base that has memory and a knowledge base that has *accountability*.

## When the Agentic and Human Curators disagree

The Agentic Curator's defaults are designed to be cautious — it never silently overrules a Human Curator. Specifically:

- An Agentic Curator cannot promote a fact above VERIFIED (max auto-score is 0.95).
- An Agentic Curator cannot delete a fact set or approved by a Human Curator.
- An Agentic Curator cannot amend the Constitution.
- If an Agentic Curator's contradiction-detection produces a CONTRADICTS edge against a Human-Curated fact, the edge is flagged at high or critical priority and surfaces in the review queue.

If you want the Agentic Curator to be more or less aggressive, that's a per-POT configuration. The defaults are conservative because of the asymmetric cost of mistakes — silently corrupting a Company Brain is much more expensive than missing a borderline promotion.

## Finding what needs curator attention

There's no single "queue" endpoint — instead, the existing edges and facts endpoints are filtered to surface what a curator should look at:

```bash
# Open contradictions: list CONTRADICTS edges that aren't resolved yet
curl "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/edges?type=CONTRADICTS&resolved=false" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -H "X-Workspace-ID: $SCIPOT_WORKSPACE"

# Promotion candidates: facts above a score threshold but not yet VERIFIED
curl "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/facts?min_pot_score=0.80&max_pot_score=0.85&status=ready" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -H "X-Workspace-ID: $SCIPOT_WORKSPACE"

# Recent contributor submissions awaiting curation
curl "https://api.scipot.ai/pots/{pot_id}/facts?sort_by=created_at&sort_order=desc&limit=50&status=ready" \
  -H "X-API-Key: $SCIPOT_KEY" \
  -H "X-Workspace-ID: $SCIPOT_WORKSPACE"
```

Combine these into your own internal curator dashboard, or use SciPot's own `GET /integrator/dashboard` and `GET /integrator/usage/summary` for a workspace-wide view of what's pending.

A good curation workflow: filter for the queue → resolve / promote / disapprove → recompute → move on. Time-boxed weekly is realistic for a small team; daily for a large one.
